Security and control

Access decided on every call.

Two layers of permission work together. Roles decide which endpoints may be called. Views decide which accounts and fields may be used. Customers add a third with their consents.

Who may do what

Views

A view decides which fields of an account each party sees, and what they may do with it. Owner, accountant, auditor and public views come built in, and banks add their own.

Roles, Entitlements and Scopes

Hundreds of Roles each open a small set of endpoints. People hold them as Entitlements, applications as Scopes, at one bank or for the whole system.

Groups

Give the same Roles to many people at once, and keep them in step when the Group changes.

Attribute-based rules

ABAC rules decide access from the attributes of the user, the account and the request.

Maker/checker

The person who creates a payment request cannot be the one who approves it.

Mandates

Corporate accounts with signatory panels and multi-signature approval.

Applications and consents

v7.0.0

Platform Apps

Mark the applications your installation runs itself. Only they may act as themselves, they declare the Scopes they need, and administrators see what is missing.

v7.0.0

Consumer Account Access

Grant an application a view of an account, so a service can act on it with its own token, without a stand-in user.

Consents

Customers decide which applications may see their data or make payments, for how long, with SCA. Unused consents expire by themselves.

Authentication

OAuth 2 and OpenID Connect

Keycloak, OBP-OIDC and public providers such as Google and Microsoft, each limited to your own client ids.

Mutual TLS

Client certificates checked at your proxy and passed on as PSD2-CERT, with the proxy itself named and trusted.

Direct Login and SIWE

Username and password for trusted apps, and Sign-In With Ethereum for wallet holders.

Client Credentials

Services sign in as themselves, with no User behind them. Each application's grant types say whether people log in through it, it gets its own token, or both.

Protection built in

  • Rate limitsPer application, per user and per IP address, shared by every instance through Redis
  • IP penaltiesAn operator can slow down or stop one address at once, and undo it as easily
  • Amounts kept exactAn amount with more decimals than its currency allows is refused, so no money disappears in rounding
  • Audit trailWho did what, when, for whom and through which application
  • Safe file handlingFiles are checked against their type, scanned, and served so they can never run as a page
  • Safe deletionCascade deletes stay off unless an operator turns them on
